Baruch

proper noun

Definition: A biblical figure traditionally identified as the scribe and companion of the prophet Jeremiah. Baruch son of Neriah is associated with the writing and preservation of Jeremiah's prophecies and became an important figure in later Jewish and apocryphal literature.

Baruch appears in the biblical Book of Jeremiah as Jeremiah's scribe and secretary. Several ancient Jewish writings are attributed to him or written under his name, including the Book of Baruch, 2 Baruch, and 3 Baruch. These works are considered pseudepigraphal or deuterocanonical depending on the text and religious tradition.

Related Terms: Jeremiah · Book of Baruch · 2 Baruch · 3 Baruch · Pseudepigrapha · Deuterocanonical Books

1 Baruch

proper noun

Definition: A deuterocanonical Jewish religious text traditionally attributed to Baruch, the scribe of the prophet Jeremiah. The book contains prayers, wisdom teachings, confessions of sin, and messages of hope addressed to the Jewish people.

1 Baruch is included in the biblical canon of the Catholic Church and several other Christian traditions but is not included in the Hebrew Bible or most Protestant Bibles.

Related Terms: Apocrypha • Baruch • Pseudepigrapha • Canon • Deuterocanonical

Bel

proper noun

Definition: An ancient Mesopotamian title meaning “lord” that was used for certain gods, especially the Babylonian god Marduk. In biblical and Jewish literature, Bel is often used as the name of a Babylonian deity.

Bel appears in ancient Mesopotamian religion as a divine title and later became particularly associated with Marduk, the chief god of Babylon. The name appears in the biblical Book of Jeremiah, where Bel is mentioned in a prophecy against Babylonian gods.

Bel is also the central figure in the deuterocanonical story Bel and the Dragon, in which the prophet Daniel exposes the deception surrounding the worship of Bel and demonstrates the powerlessness of the Babylonian idol.

Related Terms: Apocryphon, Apocryphal, Canon, Pseudepigrapha
