Ap

Apocrypha

noun • singular: Apocryphon

Definition: A collection of religious writings that are considered non-canonical or of uncertain canonical status within a particular religious tradition. The term comes from the Greek ἀπόκρυφα (apókrypha), meaning “hidden things” or “hidden writings.”

In Christian contexts, Apocrypha is commonly used to describe ancient Jewish and Christian writings that were not included in a particular biblical canon. The exact meaning of the term varies between traditions, and it is sometimes used specifically for the books accepted as Scripture in Catholic and Orthodox traditions but excluded from most Protestant Bibles.

Related Terms: Apocryphon, Apocryphal, Canon, Pseudepigrapha

Apocryphon

noun · plural: apocrypha, or apocryphons

Definition: A writing that is considered hidden, secret, or non-canonical, especially a religious text outside the accepted canon of Scripture. The term comes from the Greek apókruphon (ἀπόκρυφον), meaning “hidden” or “concealed.”

In modern scholarship, apocryphon is often used more broadly for an ancient Jewish or Christian religious writing that is not part of the biblical canon. The term is particularly common in the titles of works such as the Apocryphon of John and the Apocryphon of James.

Related Terms: Apocrypha • Pseudepigrapha • Canon • Deuterocanonical

Az

Azazel

noun

Definition: A mysterious figure or name associated with the wilderness, the scapegoat ritual, and evil or fallen angels in ancient Jewish literature.

In Leviticus 16, one of two goats used in the Day of Atonement ritual is designated "for Azazel" and sent into the wilderness. The exact meaning of Azazel in this passage has been debated, with interpretations including a supernatural being, a wilderness demon, or a place of removal.

In the Book of 1 Enoch, Azazel is portrayed as one of the fallen angels who corrupts humanity by teaching forbidden knowledge, including the making of weapons and cosmetics. He is ultimately punished and bound until the final judgment.

Related Terms: Apocryphon, Apocryphal, Canon, Pseudepigrapha
